Gongju Jemincheon

제민천

A stream-side village of hanok stays, tea houses and tiny bookshops.

StationGongju terminal + 10 min
On footAlong the stream: 15 min
Best timeOvernight stay

The story

Gongju was the capital of the ancient Baekje kingdom; Jemincheon is the stream that runs through its old town. Young returnees turned emptying houses into a “village hotel” — a village desk in one alley, guest rooms in another, breakfast laid out in the house where you sleep. Stay a night, walk to the fortress at dusk, and you’ll have Korea’s history quarter almost to yourself.

A half-day on foot

  1. 1
    11:00Lunch at Gogane Kalguksu
    Get there when it opens. It’s closed on Sundays, the museum on Mondays and the tea house on Tuesdays — so this half-day works Wednesday to Saturday.
  2. 2
    13:00Pulkkot Literature Museum
    It reopens at one, after the midday break. Free, in an old Japanese-style house, with a new wing added in 2025.
  3. 3
    14:30Tea at Lucia’s Garden
    Black tea or milk tea with a scone, in a 1960s hanok. Open 12:00–18:30, closed Tuesdays.
  4. 4
    17:00Gongju Sanseong Market
    Walk north along the stream to the market. On Friday and Saturday evenings through October 17, the Bammasil night market runs 17:00–22:00.

Pulkkot Literature Museum나태주풀꽃문학관

FREE
Poetry museum, free
Insider tip

A museum for the poet Na Tae-ju, best known for the short poem “Pulkkot” (Grass Flower). It opened in an old Japanese-style house in October 2014 and gained a new wing in July 2025; the Pulkkot literary prize is awarded from here.

Insider tip

The town cinema — opened 1963, rebuilt 1967, dark since 1995 — came back in July 2026 as a media-art hall. A 21-meter-wide LED wall on the ground floor, the old projection room recreated upstairs, and a terrace overlooking the stream.
